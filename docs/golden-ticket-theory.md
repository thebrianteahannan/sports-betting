# Golden Ticket: is the math right?

A short theory note. Not a record, not a pitch. Just whether daily Golden Tickets are +EV if the rule means what it says.

## The rule

A Golden Ticket is a single bet where:

- FanDuel price is between **-120 and +120**
- The card’s hit rate is **60% or more** (with enough games on the card to count)

The question: if you take one of these every day, are you ahead — **in theory**?

## Yes, if 60% is the true chance

Break-even chance for the price:

| Price | Need to break even |
|-------|--------------------|
| -120 (worst Golden price) | **54.5%** |
| -110 | 52.4% |
| even | 50% |
| +120 (best Golden price) | **45.5%** |

The bar is 60%. That sits **above every break-even in the band**. The smallest cushion is at -120 (about 5.5 points). The biggest is at +120 (about 14.5 points).

On a $5 bet at a true 60% hit rate:

| Price | Win pays | Expected value |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| -120 | +$4.17 | about **+$0.50** |
| even | +$5.00 | about **+$1.00** |
| +120 | +$6.00 | about **+$1.60** |

So “bet every Golden Ticket every day” is +EV **as long as 60% is real**. Repeating it daily is just repeating +EV bets.

## Where the theory can fail

The rule does not bet “true 60%.” It bets “this card’s past rate is >= 60%.” Those only match if:

1. **Past matches future** — same role, usage, and context, not a fluke stretch.
2. **No cherry-pick inflation** — scanning many props and keeping only the ones that look like 60% pulls winners from noise; the next game often regresses toward the real rate (often closer to the market’s ~52%).
3. **The sample is thick enough** — a thin record can show 60% by luck.

If the *true* rate is only ~52% and the card’s 60% was sample noise, you are **not** ahead at -120 (you need 54.5%).

## How to convince others on theory alone

Do not sell “we won lately.” Sell the **rule**:

> We only take prices where break-even is at most 54.5%, and only when our read says at least 60%. If that read is right, every ticket in the band is +EV. Daily betting is just repeating +EV bets.

That argument is airtight on paper. What others will push on is not the odds math — it is **whether the 60% is a forecast or a lookback**.

The pricing bar is correct. The whole strategy rises or falls on whether the hit rate is predictive.

## Rebuttal: “60% is only lookback”

The pushback, restated fairly:

> “60% is lookback. Lookback isn’t a forecast. So your edge is fake.”

They are right that **past rate is not next-game chance**. Concede that in one sentence, then move the fight.

**1. We never sell 60% as tomorrow’s exact probability.**  
We sell a **gate**: only bet when lookback already clears the worst Golden break-even (54.5% at -120) by a cushion. The 60% bar is not “this hits 60% tonight.” It is “this cleared juice in-sample by enough that we’re willing to take the price.”

**2. Lookback vs forecast is a false choice.**  
Every price on FanDuel is also a lookback (market history plus a model) dressed as a forecast. The fair question is: **is our filter biased, or does it still beat break-even after noise?** Not “is it lookback?”

**3. The cushion is the answer to regression.**  
People will say the rate shrinks toward the market (~52%). Agree. That is why the bar isn’t 55% — it is **60%**. You’re arguing: even if the real rate is lower than the card shows, it still has to fall a long way before -120 becomes -EV.

**4. The unit of proof is the class, not one card.**  
One player’s 6-of-10 is noisy. The honest claim is: tickets that pass this gate, as a group, should cash above the break-even of their prices. That is testable. That is what you show people — not “this prop is a 75% lock.”

**5. Rate alone is weak; rate plus why is the real forecast.**  
If all you have is hits over games, the pushback lands. The rebuttal gets teeth when the card also says **why the clear repeats** (role, usage, line shape). Lookback becomes evidence for a mechanism, not a lucky streak.

### One-liner

> We’re not forecasting 60%. We’re only taking near-even prices where the card already cleared juice by a cushion — and we grade the whole bucket, not one prop’s past.

### Do not say

- “Past results prove the next one.”
- “60% means it hits 60% tonight.”
- “Trust the sample because n is at least 5.” (Five is thin; don’t pretend otherwise.)
