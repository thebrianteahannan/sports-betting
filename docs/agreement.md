# Draft: payback, then 50/50

This is a term sheet for a lawyer. It is not a signed contract, and it is not in force. It does not create a partnership, a company, or a right to be paid. A lawyer should turn these points into the real operating agreement before anyone signs.

Revised November 12, 2026.

## Parties

- Brian Hannan.
- Anthony Hauf.

Each person represents that they are 21 or older on the day they sign. If that is not true, this draft does not apply to them, and they are not an owner, a marketer, or a payee.

## The business

Parlay of the Day (parlayoftheday.com) is a FanDuel price desk. It is live, with free membership. Members see today's and this week's games with recommended bets, build and save their own parlays from FanDuel prices, paste in a friend's bet from a screenshot, send bets to friends, and review how past bets and games played out. It does not take bets and it is not a sportsbook. A later paid membership, if one is sold, is access to the board. It is not a promise that the picks will win.

The graded record, wins and losses, stays public on the site. The signed agreement has to keep it that way.

## Ownership

1. Brian and Anthony form one company to own Parlay of the Day, most likely a Tennessee LLC. Each owns 50%.
2. Brian puts into the company everything the site runs on: the code, the parlayoftheday.com domain, the hosting account, the Supabase database, the logo, the parrot, and the member list.
3. Anthony puts in the work written under "What each person does."
4. Business money goes through a business bank account in the company's name, never a personal one.

## Order of money

1. Revenue is money the company actually collects, after refunds and payment fees.
2. Net revenue is revenue minus the written costs of running the site: hosting, data, AI tools, payment fees, ads, printing, and similar costs the two owners agree to list.
3. Net revenue goes to Brian until his initial investment is paid back in full. No owner pay or split comes first.
4. Cash either owner puts in after signing, with the other's written okay, is added to the payback list with the date and what it paid for, and is repaid the same way.
5. After the payback list is cleared, net revenue is split 50/50.
6. If the company or the site is sold before the payback is cleared, the unpaid balance comes off the top of the sale, and the rest is split 50/50.

Repayment comes only from money the company collects. It is not a debt Anthony owes from his own pocket, and it is not a promise that the site will make money. If there is no revenue, there is nothing to repay and nothing to split.

## What each person does

Brian builds and runs the site, keeps the payback list and the books, and keeps the graded record public.

Anthony's role, confirmed in writing before anyone signs: ____________________. A suggested start is growing the membership: handing out the QR cards, bringing the site to watch parties and group chats, and telling Brian what members ask for.

Neither owner places bets for members, promises wins, or calls a ticket a lock.

## Decisions

- Brian makes day-to-day calls on how the site works and looks.
- Both owners agree in writing before the company starts charging members, spends more than $________ at once, signs a contract, takes on debt, adds an owner, or sells the company or the site.
- If the owners can't agree on one of those, it doesn't happen until they do. The lawyer writes in a tie-break, such as a mediator both owners pick.

## If an owner leaves

The lawyer writes this part. Points to cover:

- An owner who stops doing their role for 90 days, or wants out, offers their share to the other owner first.
- The price is fair market value, or a formula both owners agree on when they sign.
- Brian's unpaid investment stays owed by the company, whoever owns it.
- Vesting: whether Anthony's 50% is earned over time, for example over 2 years, while he does his role.

## What this draft does not do

- It does not put the agreement in force or form the company.
- It does not authorize charging members. Charging waits on the checks in the business model.
- It does not guarantee members, revenue, or profit.
- It does not make either person a sportsbook, and it does not take bets.
- It does not include anyone under 21.

## Before it can be signed

A lawyer who knows Tennessee LLCs and gambling-related rules writes the operating agreement from these points. Both people fill in the blanks above, initial the investment table, use their legal names, and sign. Until then, Brian is repaid nothing, and there is no 50/50.
